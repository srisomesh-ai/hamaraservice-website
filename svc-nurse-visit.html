Frequently Asked Questions
Do I need a doctor's prescription for a nurse visit?›
Yes — for clinical procedures like injections, IV drips, catheter care, and wound dressing, a valid doctor's prescription is required. Share it at booking or on arrival.
Do nurses bring medicines and syringes?›
No — medicines, syringes, needles, IV fluids, and dressing materials must be arranged by the patient or family. The nurse brings clinical skills and basic equipment.
Can I get the same nurse every day?›
Yes — for regular daily or weekly bookings we assign the same nurse for continuity of care, subject to availability.
Can a nurse give an insulin injection at home?›
Yes — select "Injection / Vaccination". Insulin injections, IM, IV, and SC injections are all administered as per the doctor's prescription.
Is a full day nurse the same as a hospital attendant?›
A full day home nurse is clinically trained to handle all nursing tasks including medicines, dressings, vitals, and patient care. A general hospital attendant is not medically trained.
Can nurses handle ICU or critical care at home?›
Our nurses provide post-ICU home care, vitals monitoring, and recovery support. Active ICU-level or ventilator-dependent care is not available through this service.
What if the patient's condition worsens during the visit?›
The nurse will immediately alert the family and help contact emergency services. Our nurses are trained in first aid and basic emergency response.
Is overnight nurse service available?›
Yes — select "Night Nurse Attendant" for overnight coverage from 10 PM to 8 AM. For 24-hour coverage, book both a full day and night nurse.
